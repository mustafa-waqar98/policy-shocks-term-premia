When the Fed surprises markets, does the 10-year move on rate expectations or on the term premium?

Every Treasury yield is split each day into the short rate the market expects and the term premium it demands for duration risk. FOMC surprises, measured from futures in a 30-minute window around each statement, are traced through both.

08

Glossary

Expected short rates
The average overnight rate the market expects over a bond's life. Moves with the Fed's expected policy path.
Term premium
The extra yield investors demand for locking money up in a long bond instead of rolling short bills. Moves with inflation risk, risk appetite, supply and hedging demand.
ACM model
Adrian, Crump and Moench (2013). Estimates the split using five curve factors and the returns on bonds of different maturities. The NY Fed publishes the same series.
Policy surprise
How much rate futures out to one year moved in the 30 minutes around an FOMC statement. In a window that narrow, nothing else moves the market.
Monetary policy shock
A surprise where rates and stocks move in opposite directions: a genuine tightening or easing.
Information shock
A surprise where rates and stocks move together: the Fed revealed news about the economy.
Target and path
Target is the surprise in today's rate decision. Path is the surprise about future rates (guidance, dots) that leaves today's decision unchanged.
Local projection
For each horizon, a regression of the change in a series since the day before the meeting on the shocks. The slopes trace out the response over time.
5y5y forward
The 5-year interest rate that starts 5 years from today, implied by today's 5y and 10y yields. Shows what markets expect, and demand, for the far future.
Real yield and breakeven
TIPS pay a yield on top of inflation, so their yield is a real rate. The nominal yield minus the real yield is the breakeven: the inflation rate at which the two bonds pay the same, combining expected inflation and an inflation risk premium.
bp
Basis point, one hundredth of a percentage point.
