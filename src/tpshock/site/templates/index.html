01 · The finding

Guidance surprises turn into term premium, but only after the meeting, and only since 2008

A guidance surprise is how much the market repriced the policy rate a year out in the half hour around the statement, holding today's decision fixed. Each line is the average move in the 10-year from the close before the meeting, per 10bp of surprise. On the day, guidance moves expected rates, as it should. Over the next month the term premium takes over.

05 · Why it happens

{% if refunding %}{{ refunding.headline }}{% else %}Is it Treasury supply? The monthly data can't say{% endif %}

One explanation for the post-2008 effect is supply: the more duration private investors have to hold, the harder a change in the rate outlook pushes on the premium they want. Testing that needs supply news that arrives on a known day. The Treasury's quarterly refunding statement, which announces the sizes of the 3-, 10- and 30-year auctions at 8:30am, is that news.

The backdrop: how much duration the private market holds

Every marketable Treasury outstanding minus the Fed's holding of that exact security, weighted by duration, month by month since mid-2003.

{{ fig_supply|safe }}

Private duration supply has risen almost every year since 2008, so it moves with time itself (correlation 0.97 across post-2008 meetings). Month-by-month supply can't separate "more supply" from "later in the sample", so whether a bigger stock of duration makes guidance hit harder is still open. The guidance effect has also been larger in 2008 to 2015 than since 2016, while supply kept rising, which points away from the simplest supply story.

11 · Glossary
Expected short rates
The average overnight rate the market expects over a bond's life. Moves with the Fed's expected policy path.
Term premium
The extra yield investors demand for locking money up in a long bond instead of rolling short bills. Moves with inflation risk, risk appetite, supply and hedging demand.
ACM model
Adrian, Crump and Moench (2013). Estimates the split using five curve factors and the returns on bonds of different maturities. The NY Fed publishes the same series.
Guidance (path) surprise
The surprise about where rates go next (statement wording, dots) that leaves today's decision unchanged, measured in the 30 minutes around the statement.
Target surprise
The surprise in today's rate decision itself.
Monetary policy shock
A surprise where rates and stocks move in opposite directions: a genuine tightening or easing.
Information shock
A surprise where rates and stocks move together: the Fed revealed news about the economy.
Local projection
For each horizon, a regression of the change in a series since the day before the meeting on the shocks. The slopes trace out the response over time.
5y5y forward
The 5-year interest rate that starts 5 years from today, implied by today's 5y and 10y yields.
Real yield and breakeven
TIPS pay a yield on top of inflation, so their yield is a real rate. The nominal yield minus the real yield is the breakeven: expected inflation plus an inflation risk premium.
Refunding statement
The Treasury's quarterly announcement (February, May, August, November) of how much 3-, 10- and 30-year debt it will auction, and its plans for coupon sizes.
10-year equivalent
A way to add up bonds of different maturities by how much rate risk they carry: a 30-year bond counts as about three 10-year bonds.
bp
Basis point, one hundredth of a percentage point.
