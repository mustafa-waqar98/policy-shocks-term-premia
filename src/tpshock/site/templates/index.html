Fed shocks and the term premium

When the Fed surprises the market, does the 10-year move because the expected path of policy rates changed, or because investors demand a different premium for holding duration? This dashboard splits the Treasury curve into those two pieces every day and traces how identified FOMC shocks feed through each one.

FOMC shocks

Built from futures price changes in a 30-minute window around each announcement. The policy surprise is split by the reaction of stocks: a hawkish surprise that sends stocks down is a monetary policy shock; a hawkish surprise that sends stocks up is an information shock, the market reading the Fed as more upbeat on the economy (Jarocinski and Karadi 2020).

Scenario tool

Type in the size of a hypothetical FOMC surprise. The table shows the historical average response implied by the local projections above. A desk use: before a meeting, price what a hawkish dot plot (path shock) or a surprise cut (target shock) has typically done to the curve and to the term premium.

   

Point estimates only; responses are linear in the shock size. Uncertainty is large beyond 20 days.
